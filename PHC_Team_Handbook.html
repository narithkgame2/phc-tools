Decisions & Guidelines · Marina · Monika
Content Style Guide
How every Insights article on the website should read, and why — so anyone writing one lands on the same voice without re-deciding it each time.
→Who uses it: anyone drafting an Insights article — Marina day to day, Monika reviewing before publish.
→Where articles actually go live: propertyhubcambodia.com/admin/blog — write it anywhere, then copy-paste into that editor and publish.
The 9 rules
1
Headline: benefit-led, not a topic label
"What Foreign Buyers Actually Own" beats "Foreign Ownership Explained."
2
Answer first, context after
The direct answer goes in the first 2–3 sentences. Never make the reader scroll to find out what the article is even about.
3
A subheading every 150–250 words
Scannable, not a wall of text — a busy investor should get the shape of the answer just from skimming headers.
4
Only state what's pre-approved
Stick to the "can confidently state" list below. Hedge or leave out anything from "requires care" — never a guarantee, never a single-decimal yield figure.
5
One CTA, at the end only
Never mid-article — that reads as a pitch interrupting advice. One soft WhatsApp prompt, after the article has actually delivered value.
6
Real author, real credibility
Name, role, one line of relevant expertise — never a generic "Author." Builds the advisor identity PHC actually wants people to see.
7
Calm, expert, second person
"You" for the reader's situation, "we" for PHC. Advice from someone who's done this before — not marketing copy.
8
700–1000 words for pillar content
Long enough for real depth, short enough to respect the reader's time. If extending, add one genuinely new section — never pad to hit a word count.
9
Ranges, never false precision
"6–8% gross yield," never "7.3%." Cambodia's market doesn't support that level of certainty, and claiming it undermines trust.
What we can confidently state
Strata-title eligibility for foreigners, USD-denominated pricing, 6–8% gross rental yield (approximate range), project-specific payment plans, foreign ownership via strata title (not land freehold).
What requires care — hedge or leave out
Net yield (only with real management-fee data for that project), district-level yield claims, "delayed construction" labels (legally sensitive), any "guaranteed rental" claim without written developer documentation, and anything implying an individual condo buyer gets business-investment tax incentives (Law on Investment incentives are for registered companies, not personal buyers).
What we deliberately don't copy from competitors
Format inspiration ≠ tone to copy
realestate.com.kh's article format (Editor's Picks curation, punchier headlines, related-article links) is worth learning from. Their tone is not — it leans aspirational/opportunity-framed, which cuts against PHC's own rule of "advice, not a pitch." Also skip stats headers and view counts on our own site until we actually have the traffic to show honestly, not before.
Publishing workflow — why it's manual, on purpose
→Write the article (any format — Google Doc, plain text, whatever's easiest), then copy-paste into the existing editor at /admin/blog, add images, review, publish.
→We tested an automated "AI pushes drafts straight into the queue" pipeline. It technically worked, but felt slower in practice — the real bottlenecks (sourcing images, reviewing before publish) are identical either way, and automation only skipped the one step that was never actually slow.
→Human review before publish is not optional — the site makes real legal/financial claims, and that's exactly what the "requires care" list above exists to protect.

What Foreign Buyers Actually Own in Cambodia (And What They Don't)
Strata title lets foreigners legally own condos here outright — but the fine print decides whether that ownership is airtight or full of gaps.
M
MonikaCEO, Property Hub Cambodia — advises foreign investors on ownership structure daily

Cambodia is one of the few countries in Southeast Asia where foreigners can own property outright, not just lease it. But "foreign ownership" gets thrown around loosely, and the details decide whether your ownership is real or just paperwork that looks real.

The short answer

Foreigners can own units from the second floor upward in a co-owned building, through a legal structure called strata title. You cannot own land directly, and in most projects you cannot own a ground-floor unit.
