Why the mix changed

PHC's channels were close to 100% listing posts. That volume of listings produced roughly 2 leads across 100+ posts — not because the listings were bad, but because a listing only works on someone who's already close to ready to buy. Educational content is what builds trust with everyone else, before they're ready. That's why every two weeks now runs 7 educational posts to 3 listing posts — not the other way around.

Non-negotiable
Golden Rules

These apply to every piece of content PHC publishes — educational posts, listings, and video scripts alike. They don't bend for a good story or a slow week.

1
No guaranteed returns, no project-specific net yield
Only a market-wide gross range (6–8%) as context — never a promise, never net yield for one specific project.
2
Never say "delayed" or "behind schedule"
About any project, ours or a competitor's.
3
Never give out a developer's contact details
Every post ends with PHC's own contact block — never the developer's phone, Telegram, or sales office.
4
Never invent or fabricate details
Use only what's actually provided or verified. If a spec is missing, leave it out — don't estimate it.
5
Never create false urgency
No "only 2 units left" style lines unless it is genuinely, verifiably true right now.

The question we get asked more than any other: "Isn't buying property in Cambodia risky because foreigners can't really own anything?" Let's clear that up. ✅ Myth: Foreigners can't legally own property in Cambodia. Reality: foreigners can own strata-title condominium units outright — full legal ownership, registered in your name. Insights Articles

The educational articles that live permanently on propertyhubcambodia.com/insights — the canonical, SEO home for a topic, distinct from the shorter social posts above. All 5 Golden Rules still apply here; this section covers what's specific to long-form articles.

→Who writes it: Marina drafts, Monika reviews and publishes at propertyhubcambodia.com/admin/blog.
→How to publish: write it anywhere easy (Google Doc, plain text), copy-paste into the admin editor, add images, review, publish — see the note below on why this stays manual.
The shape every article follows
1
Headline: benefit-led, not a topic label
"What Foreign Buyers Actually Own" beats "Foreign Ownership Explained."
2
Answer first, context after
The direct answer goes in the first 2–3 sentences — never make the reader scroll to find out what it's about.
3
A subheading every 150–250 words
Scannable, not a wall of text.
4
One CTA, at the end only
Never mid-article — that reads as a pitch interrupting advice.
5
Real author, real credibility
Name, role, one line of relevant expertise — never a generic "Author." Builds the advisor identity PHC wants people to see.
6
Calm, expert, second person
"You" for the reader's situation, "we" for PHC.
7
700–1000 words
Long enough for real depth, short enough to respect the reader's time. Extending means adding one genuinely new section — never padding to hit a word count.

Legal & Tax
What Foreign Buyers Actually Own in Cambodia (And What They Don't)
Strata title lets foreigners legally own condos here outright — but the fine print decides whether that ownership is airtight or full of gaps.
M
MonikaCEO, Property Hub Cambodia — advises foreign investors on ownership structure daily

Cambodia is one of the few countries in Southeast Asia where foreigners can own property outright, not just lease it. But "foreign ownership" gets thrown around loosely, and the details decide whether your ownership is real or just paperwork that looks real.

The short answer

Foreigners can own units from the second floor upward in a co-owned building, through a legal structure called strata title — sometimes called a Certificate of Strata Title, or informally "hard title" for condos. You cannot own land directly, and in most projects you cannot own a ground-floor unit.

What strata title actually gives you
  • Full legal ownership of your specific unit, registered in your own name
  • The right to sell, rent, will, or mortgage the unit like any other asset you own
  • A shared, proportional stake in the building's common areas — lobby, pool, parking

This is not a lease, and it is not a long-term rental dressed up as ownership. It is real property ownership, recorded with the Ministry of Land Management.

What it doesn't give you
  • Ownership of the land the building sits on — that stays with a Cambodian entity, usually the developer
  • The right to a ground-floor unit in most developments, reserved for Cambodian nationals
  • Ownership the moment you pay — strata title is issued once the building is complete and registered, often a year or more after booking for off-plan projects
What a foreign buyer can and can't own in a typical Cambodian condo building
The 70/30 rule: Cambodian law caps foreign ownership at 70% of the private units in any single building — the remaining 30% must be held by Cambodian nationals or entities. This is per building, not per project.
The one document that actually matters

Ask for the Certificate of Strata Title before you sign anything, not after. A developer's sales brochure is not a legal document. A verified, government-issued strata certificate is the only thing that confirms a project is even legally structured for foreign ownership.

Certificate of Strata Title — the only document that legally confirms foreign-eligible ownership
Our take

We don't put a project in front of a foreign buyer until we've personally verified its strata-title status first. It's a slower way to work than just sending listings, but it's the difference between owning something and owning a promise.
