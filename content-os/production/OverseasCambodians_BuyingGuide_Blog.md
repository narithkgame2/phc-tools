### Introduction

If you grew up in Cambodia and now live abroad — in the US, Australia, France, or elsewhere — buying property back home is rarely just a financial decision. It's often about keeping a foothold in your home country, giving family a place to live, or simply investing somewhere you understand better than the market you emigrated to. But buying from thousands of miles away comes with its own set of questions: what can you actually own, how do you verify a developer you've never met in person, and how does the money side work when you're not there to hand over cash in an office. This guide walks through what actually matters in 2026.

### What You Can Own — And Why It's Worth Confirming Your Specific Situation

Cambodia's property ownership rules differ depending on citizenship and legal status, and this is genuinely one area where a blanket answer can mislead you — your exact situation (dual citizenship, retained Cambodian nationality, foreign passport only) changes what's available to you. Rather than guess at your specific case here, this is the first conversation worth having with an advisor before you start browsing listings — it takes minutes to clarify and changes which projects even make sense to consider. What we can say with certainty: strata-title condominiums are a well-established, verified ownership structure in Cambodia, and every project PHC recommends has its strata-title status confirmed before it goes on our list.

### Why the Market Has Matured Since You Left

For overseas Cambodians who've been away for a decade or more, it's worth knowing the market has changed. Cambodia is a fully USD-denominated market — if you're earning and saving in dollars, there's no currency conversion risk sitting between your savings and your purchase. Payment structures have also become more standardized: legitimate developers now typically ask for 10–30% at booking, with the remainder staged against construction milestones through to handover, rather than a single large payment upfront. This staged structure exists specifically to protect buyers — it ties your payments to actual progress, not just promises.

### How to Evaluate a Developer You Can't Visit in Person

This is the part that's genuinely harder from abroad, and it's where most of the real risk sits. A professional-looking website or attractive renders tell you nothing about whether a developer will deliver. What actually matters:

- **Strata-title status, verified — not assumed.** This is the legal structure that protects your ownership. Ask for confirmation, don't take it on faith.
- **Track record.** Has this developer completed projects before, and were they delivered as advertised? A first-time developer isn't automatically a red flag, but it changes how much scrutiny the deal deserves.
- **Payment plan structure.** Staged payments tied to construction milestones are standard for legitimate projects. A demand for a large lump sum upfront, with no staging, is worth questioning.

None of this is easily checkable from a browser in another country — it's the kind of due diligence that benefits from someone physically in Phnom Penh doing the checking before you're emotionally invested in a specific unit.

### Gross Yields, Realistically

If part of your interest is rental income rather than a place for family, it's worth having realistic expectations going in. Gross rental yields across the Phnom Penh market broadly range 6–8%, though this varies significantly by district and project — and gross is not the same as net once management fees and vacancy are factored in. Be skeptical of anyone quoting a specific guaranteed return; legitimate advisors talk in ranges, not promises.

### What This Means for Your Next Step

Buying property back home doesn't have to be a leap of faith just because you're not physically there to check things yourself. The gap between "interested" and "confident" is usually just a handful of honest questions answered by someone who isn't trying to close you quickly. Start with your specific ownership situation, confirm the developer's paperwork before anything else, and treat staged payment plans as the norm, not a red flag.

### Conclusion

Cambodia's property market in 2026 is more transparent and more accessible to overseas buyers than it was even a few years ago — but distance still means the due-diligence burden is real.
